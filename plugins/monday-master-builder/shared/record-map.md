# Record map

The one home for the client record: its files, owners, budgets, schemas, vocabularies and every string the scripts look for. It carries A§7 of the architecture as amended (A12 to A17), the report and status shapes of A§5.4 and the type vocabulary of A§8.3; WP2 decisions of 2026-10-14 applied (build/wp2/DECISIONS.md). Skills, templates and scripts link here and never restate it; `record.py check` looks for the strings spelled here; `tools/lint_plugin.py` asserts the pinned sections that a script embeds agree with it. Paths are relative to the record unless said otherwise; every example value is fictional and every owner is a role.

## Kinds, budgets and the two levels

Kinds: **current** (rewritten under the sha guard), **history** (only grows, newest first), **snapshot** (frozen when written; corrections appended as dated notes), **raw** (saved once, never edited), **machine** (JSON, script-only), **scratch** (`.work/`, never archived). Budgets are `record.py check` warnings unless marked blocking. One record per client (A14): the folder `<MMB_HOME>/clients/<slug>/` holds the client — research, calls, documents, the meeting register, the account binding and what was read from the account, the live map, the lease and the record-level close-out. Each piece of work is a strand under `strands/<id>/` and holds what dies with it. Calls and documents stay at record level because one input routinely serves two strands and is never copied; `discovery.md` is strand level because each strand is graded on its own evidence, with carry-over between strands through `SUMMARY.md` only.

## Naming and references

- **Slug**: lowercase ASCII with hyphens, at most 40 characters, legal suffixes dropped, never renamed; it is the folder name, the folder name under `archive/` after the record close and, on the GitHub archive path only, the repository name `client-<slug>` under the GitHub account in `local/settings.json`.
- **Strand id**: `NNN-<topic>`, a zero-padded ordinal assigned by `record.py strand open`, monotonic per record, never reused and never renamed, plus a topic slug of at most 24 lowercase ASCII characters. The ordinal alone (`001`) is the **short id** used in log entries, lease records, element keys and version references; the full id is the folder name.
- **Dated files**: `YYYY-MM-DD-<topic>`, with `-2`, `-3`, … for a second and later file the same day, assigned by `record.py add-transcript` and `add-document`. A transcript and its summary share one stem; a document and its snapshot share one stem. A package id is `pkg-YYYY-MM-DD`, with `-2` likewise.
- **Versions** are quoted strings (`"v0.1"`), unambiguous inside a strand. Every reference that leaves a strand folder — the live map, `account.json`, a log entry, another strand's change table, `SUMMARY.md` — is **fully qualified**: a version as `001/v0.1`, an element as `001:B1`, a frozen revision as `001/v0.1r4`; inside the strand's own ledger a frozen element reads `v0.1r4#B1`. `record.py check` blocks on a qualified reference that does not resolve. The one qualified key a strand's ledger accepts is a `parent_key` for a parent another strand built (`001:W1`), which must be a row of `account.json.live_objects` whose `monday_id` equals the row's `parent_id`; every other row key in a ledger is bare.
- **Element keys**, bare inside a strand's own blueprint and ledger: `W1` workspace, `F1` folder, `B1` board, `B1.G2` group, `B1.C3` column, `B1.V1` view, `B1.FM1` form, `B1.SUB` subitems companion, `B1.IB1` an item batch of seeded sample rows (at most 20 items), `D1` doc, `DB1` dashboard, `DB1.W2` widget, `A1` automation, `PF1` portfolio, `PJ1` project, `WF1` workflow, `AG1` agent, `B1.AI1` AI column, `VB1` Vibe app, `SQ1` sequence, `WH1` webhook, `WF1.PUB` and `VB1.PUB` a publication, `AG1.ACT` and `SQ1.ACT` an activation, `SOP.doc` the doc copy, `U1` a UI step. A17 (2026-09-13) makes AI agents, AI columns and Vibe apps platform elements, and A18 brings workflows, sequences and webhooks in beside them.
- **Session label**: minted by `record.py lease take` as `YYYYMMDD-HHMM-xxxx`, the local date and minute of the take and four characters from `a-z0-9` drawn at random and re-drawn until the label equals none in the lock's `history`, for example `20261015-1330-k7q2`; two takes on one day never collide. A label is never free text and is compared as an exact string. Links inside the record are relative only.

## The reading contract

A chat working strand N reads `CLIENT.md`, the newest `log.md` entries, its own `STRAND.md` and strand files, `live/live-map.md`, and the `SUMMARY.md` of any strand its work touches. It never reads another strand's `discovery.md`, `blueprint.md`, `build.json`, versions or calls unless the user names one, and the front door does not offer them. A new strand may also read `CLIENT.md` with `prior_builds`, `account.json`, `meetings.md`, `research/`, every call and document snapshot, and the account live, read-only. It does not write into another strand's folder, re-open another strand's version, edit a live-map row it did not build (a `client` or `native` row apart, which no strand owns and which any strand's session may set from a client quote or a user decision, as `live-map/1` says), or adopt another strand's unverified objects during recovery.

## Section owners and the strand index

`CLIENT.md` body blocks, one owner each: folder map (engagement); strand index (`record.py`, read by every skill); `prior_builds` (engagement, appended by a strand close: per strand the archive location inside the record, the live objects we own with fully qualified keys, the allowance they consume, the client's quoted change-cost expectation and the quoted do-not-disturb list, kept even when transcripts are not); stakeholders and decision roles in the client's words (discovery-check, record-wide); recent research (engagement); links. `STRAND.md` body blocks: headline and top pains in the client's words (call-intake); verdict and top gaps (discovery-check); version and state with the approved revision's short sha (account-build and solution-audit); what this build rests on (build-readiness); next step (whichever skill set it); open commitments (call-intake); open overrides (any skill, append-only); open UI steps (account-build); the `## Paused` block (`record.py`: what is unresolved while the strand is paused, by the user or by a park at a lease break, and `none (resumed YYYY-MM-DD)` after `strand resume`); links. A meeting chat rewrites its own block only, through `record.py guard`. The `STRAND.md` template carries the headings `## What this build rests on` and `## Open commitments`. The first holds `- clearance: <the clearance string>`, then `- assumptions: <n> in readiness.md, <n> structural`, then one line per structural assumption (a `leaning`, `board-architecture` or `automation-condition`) as `readiness.md` writes it, without its question. The second holds one line per commitment still open, newest call first, `- <what> · owner: <role> · when: <date or unknown> · from: calls/<stem>.md`; a line leaves it only on evidence that the commitment was kept.

The strand index is one table under the heading `## Strand index` in the body of `CLIENT.md`, with this header line, a separator and one row per strand ordered by short id, the first cell being the short id; `record.py` writes it in the same locked step as any change to a `STRAND.md`, and also sets `strands_total`, `strands_open` and `current_strand`. `record.py check` reads only the lines of that block whose first cell is three digits and compares them with the folders on disk: it blocks when the set of rows or a counter differs, and warns when a title, kind, state, version, version state or clearance differs from its `STRAND.md`; `live objects` and `last activity` are display cells and are never compared:

| 001 | Service desk intake | platform | active | v0.1 | approved | cleared to build | 0 | 2026-09-12 |

## Schemas

Front matter is flat `key: "value"` lines, every value a single-line quoted string, parsed without PyYAML, with `schema` as the first field. Inside a value `\"` is a quote and `\\` a backslash and every other character is literal; a line may end with a `# comment` after the closing quote, and a whole line may be a comment. The writer changes only the quoted value of the line it sets, keeps every other line (comments, blank lines, unknown keys, order) byte for byte, and appends a new key as `key: "value"` just before the closing `---`; a file with a parse error is refused. A skill or script that meets a schema newer than its own refuses to write. Counts and flags in front matter are strings (`"1"`, `"yes"`); in JSON they are JSON types, and every monday id is a string, of digits with no leading zero where the id is numeric, so one id has one spelling: `ledger.py` refuses another spelling in a flag or under an id key of a file it is handed. Text the scripts compose is written as UTF-8 without a BOM and with LF line endings; JSON is written as `json.dumps(data, indent=2, ensure_ascii=False)` plus a final LF, key order kept; raw inputs and frozen copies are byte copies. Machine timestamps in JSON are UTC, `YYYY-MM-DDTHH:MM:SSZ`; `updated` in front matter (`YYYY-MM-DDTHH:MM`), log headers (`YYYY-MM-DD HH:MM`) and dates (`YYYY-MM-DD`) are the writing machine's local time with no zone.

### `client/2`, the front matter of `CLIENT.md`

Identity and target: `slug`, `name`, `account_kind` (`own` | `client` | `sandbox`) and `build_target` (`own-account` | `client-account` | `none`), the two agreeing (own with own-account, client with client-account, sandbox with none). After them and optional, `build_target_reason`: why the target is what it is, in one line. `record.py init` writes the reason of the default, the user's own account until the user names another target, and prints the target and the reason; a skill that records the user's choice of another target rewrites the reason through the guard. A record written without it reads as having none and stays `client/2`, and the readiness check then gives the default as the reason. Binding, each `"unbound"` until `ledger.py bind` writes `account.json.account` and mirrors it here, a difference being blocking: `monday_account_id`, `monday_account_slug`, `monday_bound_user_id`, `monday_bound_at`; `account_kind`, `build_target` and `client_workspaces` are mirrored from `account.json` the same way. Tier: `monday_tier` (`"unknown"` until read), `monday_tier_read_at` (`"never"` until read), `monday_tier_source` (`preflight` | `client statement` | `unread`). Scope and state: `products_in_scope` (comma-joined from `core`, `crm`, `service`, the union across open strands), `client_workspaces` (`"none"`, or `product:id` pairs joined by `;`, `"none"` matching an empty block in `account.json`), `record_state` (`active` | `closing` | `closed`), `strands_open`, `strands_total`, `current_strand` (a short id or `"none"`), `live_build` (`"no"` until anything of ours is live), `lease` (`"none"`, or `"<short id> <version> (<session_label>)"`, for example `"001 v0.1 (20261015-1330-k7q2)"`, mirroring the lock; a disagreement is blocking). Activity: `last_input` (`"none"`, or the stem of the newest call, document or email saved), `last_researched` (`"never"` or a date). The three open A11 fields stay at record level, asked once per client at the moment each first matters and never at setup: `transcripts_at_close` (`unanswered` | `exclude` | `archive`) and `transcript_redaction` (`unanswered` | `none` | `offered-per-call` | `always`), asked before the first real transcript or document is saved [A11-3]; `sample_data_in_account` (`unanswered` | `allowed` | `not-allowed` | `not-needed`), asked before the first revision in any strand plans sample rows [A11-2]. Then `ai_notice` (`not-yet` | `not-needed` | `given YYYY-MM-DD`) and `updated` (the local minute). Last and optional, `former_names`: the client's earlier names, each one line with no `;`, joined by `; ` in the order they were dropped, empty or absent when the client has had no other name; a record written without it reads as having none, so it stays `client/2`. `record.py client rename --name "<new>"` is the one command that changes `name`: it appends the old name to `former_names` once, never drops an entry, refuses a name the client has or has had, and logs a record-level `decision` entry with key `rename`; the slug never changes. The user may also add an old name by hand. `leak_lint.py` reads every former name as one of the record's own names, so the field holds only this client's earlier names. `record.py check` blocks on a missing field (`former_names` and `build_target_reason` apart), a value outside its set (for `former_names`, an empty name between the `;`), a target that does not match the kind and a mirror that differs, and warns on an unknown field. It blocks on an A11 field left `unanswered` at the moment it matters, which it reads from three signals, and is silent at every other moment; it never fills one in. A11-3 has arrived once any save entry in `log.md` has `fixture: no` (`add-transcript` and `add-document` themselves refuse a save without `--fixture` while `transcripts_at_close` or `transcript_redaction` is `unanswered`). A11-2 has arrived when `build_target` is `client-account` and a platform or hybrid strand's `solution/blueprint.md` has `sample_rows: "planned"`. ### `strand/1`, the front matter of `STRAND.md`

### `summary/1`, `scope/1`, `version/1` and the rest

`SUMMARY.md` (`summary/1`): `strand`, `title`, `kind`, `versions` (the released versions joined by `, `), `closed`, `summary_current_to` (the newest released version, or `none`), `budget_lines` (`"80"`); then nine blocks under these headings, in order: `## What the work was` (what the work was and why it opened); `## Pains it served` (one line each, no quote over seven words); `## What was built` (qualified keys to monday ids grouped by board, each pointing at its live-map row); `## What the client confirmed` (pointers to the call summaries); `## What must not be broken` (and where that came from); `## Cost of change, in the client's words`; `## Ownership and switch-off at close`; `## Backlog seeds carried forward`; and `## Dates` (opened, shown, signed off, released and closed). A column or group of a native the strand did not install keeps every rule. `parent_key` apart, every field above is written by the `ledger.py` command that handles the row (`approval`, `intent`, `record`, `verify`, `depart`), and `digest` is defined once: `prefix:` followed by the first eight hex digits of the sha256 of `json.dumps(fields, sort_keys=True, separators=(",", ":"), ensure_ascii=False)` in UTF-8, where `fields` is the kind's identity fields with every absent one null, ids as strings, id lists sorted and object lists sorted by `id`, and `prefix` the row's bare key in lower case (`b1:`, `ag1:`); `ledger.py` computes every digest itself from the read it is handed. **`irreversible_yes[]`**: `{key, what, at, mode, approval_ref, evidence, log_ref, run_id, operation, used_by}`, written by `ledger.py irreversible-yes` (and, for a `card` entry, by `intent`), with `mode` `card` | `per-item` | `grouped`, `evidence` the containment evidence an archive needs and `log_ref` the header line of the log entry, or null, `run_id` the run the yes was given in, `operation` the operation it is for (`create` unless `--operation` names another), and `used_by` the `attempt.started_at` of the `intent` it covered, null until then. A yes covers one intent of its own run and operation, matched by `run_id` and then by time; only an `intent --retry` of that same attempt uses it again, and an archive's evidence is read from its own yes. **`departures[]`**: `{key, version, what, error_code, attempts[], decision}`, `decision` one of `ui-step U<n>`, `blueprint-revision` and `later-retry`, the first with the number of the UI step `depart` appended in the same write. **`ui_steps[]`**: `{key, what, owner, target_version, status}` with `status` `open` | `done`; a key minted at build time takes the next free `U<n>` after the blueprint's.

Status moves, and the command that makes each; every other move is refused, and `removed` is final. `not-checkable` and `needs-user` never make a row `verified`, so no live-map row is created for it:

| From | To | By |
|---|---|---|
| `planned` | `attempting` | `intent` |
| `planned` | `verified`, `mismatch` | `verify`, on a `pre-existing-referenced` row only |
| `planned` | `attempting` | `intent --operation update`, on a column or group row of a native the strand installed (A38) |
| `attempting` | `attempting` | `intent --retry`, a new attempt replacing `attempt`, only after a reconcile over a read made after the attempt began classes the row `retry` (for a kind no inventory carries, after such a reconcile has run); a `depart` of an `attempting` row needs the same |
| `attempting` | `written` | `record` with an id (or item ids) |
| `attempting` | `removed` | `record` after an `archive` intent |
| `written` | `verified` | `verify --result match` |
| `written` | `mismatch` | `verify --result mismatch` |
| `written` | `written` | `verify --result not-checkable` or `needs-user`: the verify block is recorded, the status stays |
| `mismatch` | `attempting` | `intent --retry`, once, with the one different call form; never for an automation, which departs |
| `verified` | `attempting` | `intent --operation update` (the version joins `changed_in[]`), or `--operation archive` |
| `removed` | `removed` | `verify`, recording the archive's read-back |

**Ledger write orders.** Every `ledger.py` command takes the record mutex once, at its start, and keeps it until it has printed; every file it reads or writes is read and written inside that one hold, and the lease primitives it calls re-enter it. A command that writes more than one file writes in this order, the lock always last:

| Command | Writes, in order | On a failure after the first write |
|---|---|---|
| `run start` (answer `no`) | 1 `lease_refresh` with the new run id (the primitive writes the `account.json` mirror, then the lock); 2 the strand's `build.json` (the run entry, `lease_epoch_at_run`) | exit 3 with `committed`; `next` is the same call with `--run-id <id>`, which `run start` accepts only while the lock carries that run id and `build.json` has no run of that id |
| `run start` (answer `yes`) | 1 `build.json` (the run entry, state `ended`); 2 `lease_release` | `committed`; `next` is `record.py lease release --epoch <e> --label <l>` |
| `run end` | 1 `lease_fence` (moves the heartbeat; not a commit); 2 `account.json` (`live_objects`, `updated_at`); 3 `live/live-map.md`; 4 `build.json` (the run entry ended); 5 `lease_release` | `committed`; `next` is the same `run end` call, which is safe to repeat: when the named run is already `ended` and the lock still carries it, steps 2 and 3 are rewritten (no change when equal) and the lease is released |
| `verify` (a native install's `match`, A38) | 1 `account.json` (`guard_index`); 2 the strand's `build.json` (the row) | `committed`; `next` is the same `verify` call, which enters the native as it stands and writes the row |
| `live-map set` | 1 `account.json` (the row); 2 `live/live-map.md` | `committed`; `next` is `ledger.py live-map`, which renders the map from `live_objects` |
| `reconcile --inventory` | 1 `.work/strands/<id>/live-inventory.json` (the copy); 2, with `--classify-only`, the holder's `build.json` | `committed`; `next` is the same call without `--inventory`, which reads the copy |

`bind`, `workspaces` and `native-map` write `account.json` alone, `live-map` writes `live/live-map.md` alone, `snapshot` writes one new file, and every other writing command writes one `build.json`, a native install's `verify` apart. A single write that fails is exit 3 naming its file, with nothing committed. A failure after a committed write raises exit 3 and prints `committed`: `{command, written[], not_written[], lease, run_id, next, shas}`, `shas` holding the new sha256 of each file written but the lock, `next` being the exact call that completes it. A crash in `run end` before step 4 leaves a `running` run with a stale heartbeat, the ordinary recovery trigger, and a crash between steps 4 and 5 is met by `reconcile --classify-only`, which also marks an `ended` run `interrupted` when the lock still carries it.

**Reconcile classes.** `reconcile` gives every row of the strand at most one class and every unclaimed live object one. A live match of a row, per kind: a `workspace` by id, else by name; a `board`, `subitems`, `portfolio` or `project` row a live board with the row's name, workspace (or, inside a folder row, that folder's id as `board_folder_id`), the matching type or key and a state other than `deleted`; a `folder` or `doc` by name and workspace; an `automation` by the parent board's id and a title equal to the row's name after whitespace is collapsed and case folded, or a live title of at least 40 characters that the row's name begins with; a `dashboard` by stored id only; an `agent` by name; a `vibe_app` by name and bound boards; a `workflow` by name and workspace; a `publication` or `activation` row by its parent's live entry. Groups, columns, views, forms, widgets, item batches, AI columns, sequences and webhooks are in no inventory and are listed under `not_in_inventory` with their status and the read to make. A live object is claimed when its id is a row's `id`, `item_ids`, `companions` value or doc container, a `live_objects` `monday_id`, or in `native_board_index`.

| Class | Test |
|---|---|
| `adopt` | an `attempting` row whose recorded id (read from an error) is live, or which has no id and exactly one unclaimed live match created at or after `attempt.started_at`; an adopted automation, a workflow and any match whose `created_at` the read lacks carries `confirm: true`, and an agent carries `needs_read: "manage_agent get"` |
| `retry` | an `attempting` row with a recorded id that is not live, or with no unclaimed live match created at or after its attempt started |
| `duplicate` | an `attempting` row with two or more unclaimed live matches created at or after its attempt started; every candidate id is listed, and each but the one the user keeps becomes an archive proposal (a deactivation proposal for an agent, a switch-off step for an automation), each with its own yes |
| `partial` | a row whose live object exists but is not complete: a `written` or `mismatch` row whose id is live; a `verified` row whose live digest is unchanged while a row under it is not `verified`; a Vibe app whose live status is not `READY` |
| `changed-since-verified` | a `verified` row whose live object is present and whose identity digest differs from `verify.digest` |
| `unrecorded-extra` | an unclaimed live object in the strand's target workspaces, or an unclaimed agent or Vibe app created at or after the strand's earliest attempt; one that predates the attempt of a row it matches is listed with `predates_attempt: true` and never offered for adoption |
| `missing` | a `written`, `mismatch` or `verified` row whose id is not live, or is live in state `deleted` |

A `verified` row with nothing changed and nothing incomplete is listed under `unchanged`, `planned` rows under `not_started`, and `departed` and `removed` rows under `not_reconciled`; none of these takes a class. The output lists elements in this order: automations, agents, Vibe apps, workflows, then every other kind in key order, a kind's unrecorded extras after its rows.

**What `ledger.py check` blocks and warns on.** `check` reads `account.json`, every strand's `build.json`, `live/live-map.md` and every frozen file an approval or a ref names, inside the mutex, and writes nothing; it does not take part in the pinned vocabulary below. It blocks on: a schema unknown or newer; a block or field of this schema missing or of the wrong type, the optional fields read by their defaults; a value outside its vocabulary for `type`, `origin`, `status`, `state`, `verify.result`, `approval_mode`, `irreversible_yes[].mode`, run `kind` and `state`, `constraint`, UI step `status` and the reconcile classes; an id stored as a number; a parent key that does not resolve or a `parent_id` that disagrees; `prior_state` where the origin forbids it or missing where it needs it, and an `other-strand-modified` row without `source_strand` or `live_key`; a row without an `approval_ref` or with one that resolves to no entry, and an `irreversible_yes` entry whose key is listed nowhere or whose ref does not resolve; an approval whose `sha256` is not its frozen file's; the key half of a `blueprint_ref` or `prompt_ref` that the frozen table does not hold; an `other-strand-modified` row whose live row is `do-not-change`; a `workspace` or `workspace_scope` id outside `client_workspace_ids`, or an account-level row without a scope; a `verified` row whose `verify.result` is not `match`; two `running` runs in one strand; a live-map row and a `live_objects` row that differ in any column, or a row on one side only; an `account_id_at_bind` that is not `account.id`; a build target outside the client's workspaces; two `live_objects` rows with one `monday_id` and different constraints; and an `own-account-poc` approval on a record whose build target is not `own-account` or whose version's purpose is not `proof-of-concept`. It warns on a frozen file without the element-key table, a native index older than `record_provenance.staleness_budget_days`, and a `running` run whose `heartbeat_at` is more than 60 minutes old. Its output is `{"ok", "command": "check", "findings": {"blocking": [], "warnings": []}, "shas": {...}}`, exit 1, 2, 0 or 3.

### `live-map/1`, `live/live-map.md`

Front matter `schema`, `client`, `rows`, `updated`. One table with this header line and one row per verified live object, or departed one still in monday, mirrored field for field by `account.json.live_objects`; a row present on one side and absent on the other is blocking:

```
| key | monday id | kind | workspace | built by | state | constraint | source | dependants | last verified |
| 001:B1 | 1234567890 | board | 5550001 | 001/v0.1 | active | change-with-own-yes | calls/2026-09-10-demo.md@00:41:12 | 001:A1, 001:B1.V1 | 2026-09-15 |
```

`kind` is the object type token of the element-key list, never prose (`ai_column`, `vibe_app`); `workspace` is the workspace id, or for an account-level object `account: <id>; <id>` with its scope; `built by` is a qualified version, or `client` or `native`; `state` is `active` | `inactive` | `archived` | `removed-by-platform`, `inactive` being an agent, workflow, sequence or automation that exists and does not run; `constraint` is `do-not-change` | `change-with-own-yes` | `free`; `source` is a client quote with its call location, a user decision with its log reference, `pending strand close` (the interim value `ledger.py run end` writes) or `migration default, not confirmed with the client`. A map written with the nine columns of `live-map/1` before `workspace` was added reads with `workspace` empty and is rewritten with ten at the next write. Rows are sorted by key, a pipe inside a cell is written `\|`, and `name_at_build` and `digest` stay in `account.json.live_objects` only. `ledger.py run end` writes a row for each verified object of the strand except an item batch, with `built by` `native` or `client` for a pre-existing object, `native` when its id is in `native_board_index`, and `native` for a native the strand installed (A38), which no strand owns. It writes one too for each departed row that holds a monday id, unless the row modifies another strand's object, so that the audit and the switch-off step still see what the build left in monday: `state` as last read, or `active`, `departed` set and `last_verified` null, and the map's `last verified` cell reading `departed, 001/v0.1: ui-step U1`, the departure's version and decision; a later run end that finds the row verified again drops `departed`. A row created at a run end takes `change-with-own-yes` and the source `pending strand close`, and `owner_strand` the strand, or null on a `client` or `native` row, which no strand owns, unless another live row already holds its monday id: it then takes the strictest constraint of those rows, with the source `as <key>: <their source>`, and `intent` refuses a write to an object, its parent or a referenced object that any live row marks `do-not-change`, whatever key mirrors it; a later run end refreshes `monday_id`, `kind`, `workspace`, `state`, `digest`, `last_verified` and `dependants` and keeps `name_at_build`, `owner_strand`, `introduced_in`, `constraint` and `constraint_source`. An object a later run archives keeps its row with the state `archived`. Only `ledger.py live-map set` changes a constraint. On a row a strand built, only that strand sets it; on a `client` or `native` row any strand's session may, and only with a source that is a client quote with its location (a `calls/` or `inputs/` file and a location of its grammar, such as `calls/2026-09-10-demo.md@00:41:12` or `inputs/2026-09-02-price-list.md §3.2`) or a user decision with the header line of its log entry; `check` blocks a row a strand built that has no `owner_strand`.

### `lock/2`, `.work/lock.json`, the lease

Written only by the lease primitives in `mmb_common.py`, which `record.py lease` and `ledger.py` call; a take, a refresh, a release, a break and a repair are state changes and the file is never deleted. Fields, in this order: `schema`, `kind` (`build`), `epoch` (an integer, `0` before the first take), `strand`, `strand_title`, `version`, `run_id`, `session_label`, `taken_at`, `heartbeat_at` (each a string while `state` is `held` and `null` otherwise), `state` (`none` | `held` | `released` | `broken`), `history[]`; there is no `surface`. Every take and every break increments the epoch by one, with no special case: breaking a lease held at epoch E leaves the lock at E+1 in state `broken`, and the next take, the taker's on a park or the fresh holder session's on a resume, makes it E+2. A heartbeat older than 60 minutes reads stale; nothing expires on its own. A history entry is appended on a take, a release, a break, a park and a repair, never on a refresh: `{event, at, epoch, strand, version, session_label, run_id, by, choice, reconcile, note}` with `event` `taken` | `released` | `broken` | `parked` | `repaired`, `at` in UTC, `by` the acting session's label (the minted label on `taken`, the holder's on `released`, `null` on `broken`, `parked` and `repaired`, because the acting session holds none), `choice` `resume` | `park` on a break and `null` otherwise, `reconcile` the class counts of the classify-only run on a break and `null` otherwise, and `note` on a break and a park the taking strand and the user's quoted words (`for 002: "..."`), on a repair what it was rebuilt from and the user's words, and `null` otherwise. A `broken` and a `parked` entry also carry `for_strand`, the strand the break was run for, and a `repaired` entry carries `for_strand`, the strand the repaired lock is kept for, or `null`.

**Whose take comes next.** While the lock is `broken`, its newest history entry keeps the lease for one strand, and `lease take` refuses every other: after a break with `resume`, the holder's strand (its fresh session runs the ordinary recovery); after a park, the `for_strand` of the `parked` entry; after a repair, the `for_strand` of the `repaired` entry. A reservation for a strand that has since closed lapses. `record.py lease status` and `status --json` show it as `reserved_for`.

**Writes and repairs.** A primitive writes both mirrors first and the lock last, so a write that fails leaves the lease as it was and the same call can be retried. Where a `ledger.py` command writes the lock and other files in one call, its order and its retry are in Ledger write orders, under `build/2`. A take, a release, a break and a repair back the lock up first (`.work/backup/.work/lock.json.<stamp>`); a refresh does not. The fence that `ledger.py` calls before every account-affecting write passes only while a run has started under the lease and is the presented one, and when it passes it moves the heartbeat in the same hold. A run id enters only the lease it started under. `record.py lease sync` rewrites both mirrors from the lock, the lock unchanged; `record.py lease repair --user-yes "<the user's words>"` rebuilds a missing, torn or malformed lock in place, never by a delete: one epoch above every epoch the newest sound lock backup and the `account.json` mirror know, with that backup's history and a `repaired` entry, in state `broken` kept for the strand the mirror names as holder (or the backup kept it for), else `released`, and a record-level `lease` log entry. The two mirrors, `lease` in `CLIENT.md` and `account.json.guard_index.lease`, are written in the same call, and `record.py check` blocks when either disagrees with the lock (naming `lease sync`) and when the lock's shape is not the one above (naming `lease repair`).

### `record.py status --json`

One JSON object, the front door's one read and the graders' too; front-matter values come back as the strings they are, computed values as JSON types:

```
{"schema": "status/1", "generated_at": "...", "plugin_version": "0.1.0", "record_schema": "client/2", "schema_newer": false,
 "record": {every client/2 field by name},
 "strands": [{every strand/1 field by name, "qualified_version": "001/v0.1", "purpose": "proof-of-concept", "shown": ["2026-09-14"], "signed_off": null,
              "open_versions": ["v0.1"], "live_objects": 0,
              "last_activity": "2026-09-12", "run": {"id": "...", "state": "running", "heartbeat_at": "...", "heartbeat_age_minutes": 4}}],
 "lease": {"held": true, "state": "held", "epoch": 7, "strand": "001", "strand_title": "...", "version": "v0.1", "session_label": "20261015-1330-k7q2",
           "run_id": "...", "taken_at": "...", "heartbeat_at": "...", "heartbeat_age_minutes": 22, "fresh": true, "reserved_for": null},
 "interrupted": [{"strand": "001", "run_id": "...", "version": "v0.1"}],
 "findings": {"blocking": [{"file": "...", "field": "...", "problem": "...", "expected": "..."}], "warnings": []}}
```

`schema_newer` is true when any record file carries a schema newer than the plugin's, and the object is still returned. `purpose` is the current version's, `shown` its shown dates (possibly empty) and `signed_off` its date or null. `open_versions` lists the strand's versions whose `version.md` `state` is not `released` and whose `close_reason` is `none`, sorted by number; `live_objects` counts the `account.json` `live_objects` entries whose `owner_strand` is the strand and whose `state` is `active` or `inactive`; `last_activity` is the date of the newest log entry at the strand's position across the record log and the strand's rolled logs, or null; `run` is the newest `build.json` `runs[]` entry in state `running`, as `{id, state, heartbeat_at, heartbeat_age_minutes}`, and null when there is none; an `interrupted` run appears only in the top-level `interrupted` list, as `{strand, run_id, version}`. `--strand <id>` returns the same object with one entry in `strands`. `lease.held` is false, with `state` `none`, `released` or `broken` and the last `epoch`, when nothing is held, and `heartbeat_age_minutes` and `fresh` are then null. `lease.reserved_for` is the strand whose take alone comes next after a break or a repair (see the lock), else null.

## Version states

Per strand; a `non-platform` strand has none. `planned` → `approved` → `building` → `built` → `audited` → `released`, with `fixing` between an audit that fails and the retry; `shown` and `signed-off` are events recorded in `version.md`, never states. The files `record.py check` requires, relative to the strand:

| State | Files required |
|---|---|
| `planned` | `solution/blueprint.md`; `versions/<v>/version.md` with `revision` not `none` and a `## Scope` heading |
| `approved` | `versions/<v>/blueprint-r<N>.md` whose sha256 equals `version.md` `sha256`; the approval entry in `build.json.approvals` for that version and revision; an `approval` log entry at the strand position with that version |
| `building` | a `runs[]` entry in `running` for the version with `lease_epoch` and `another_build_running` set; `.work/lock.json` held by this strand; `change_check.for_strand` in `account.json` this strand; `preflight.version` this version |
| `built` | a `## Build` heading in `version.md`; a `build` log entry |
| `fixing` | an `audit*.md` with a `## Findings` heading |
| `audited` | the newest `audit*.md`, with `## Write detection` and `## Documents appendix` headings and a last non-empty line `cleared to show` or `not cleared to show`; the appendix holds each document as a fenced block marked `graphql` or `gql`, or as a string under a key named `query` inside a fenced `json` block, and any other fenced block there is refused as unreadable by `ledger.py compare` |
| `released` | `versions/<v>/build.json`, `backlog.md` and `log.md`; from `v1.0` a `signed-off` event in `version.md` |

A version's required files are those of its current state's row and of the rows it has passed through, except that `building` and `fixing` are checked only while current. A missing file is a warning in every state but `released`, where it is blocking, and `record.py version release` refuses on any missing file of the rows it needs; condensed current files are not checked by a script. `presented` in `export.md` is accepted for a v0.x export and blocks v1.0.

## The log

`log.md` (`log/1`, front matter `schema` and `client`) is one record-wide history, newest first, entries never edited, written only by `record.py log`, which re-reads the file and inserts under the header. An entry is this header line, then at most five lines:

```
## YYYY-MM-DD HH:MM · <strand or -> · <type> · <version or -> · <key or session label>
```

The strand position is the short id, or `-` for a record-level event (a binding, a research pass, a lease break, a record close); the version is bare because the strand position qualifies it. Types, 44 in all, embedded in `record.py` between pinned markers so that `tools/lint_plugin.py` can assert the list agrees with this one: `created`, `binding`, `workspace`, `research`, `call`, `document`, `email`, `conflict`, `readiness`, `override`, `superseded` (only the same commitment from the same speaker inside one call; any other pair is a `conflict`, with no arithmetic between the two), `scope`, `blueprint`, `approval`, `change-check`, `preflight`, `build`, `departure`, `irreversible`, `automation`, `workflow`, `agent`, `ai-column`, `vibe`, `sequence`, `webhook`, `write-failed`, `recovery`, `audit`, `write-detected`, `shown`, `signed-off`, `lint`, `version`, `release`, `strand`, `lease`, `live-map`, `summary`, `api-surprise`, `decision`, `commitment`, `closeout`, `archive`. A save of a call, document or email is one entry at strand position `-` and version `-`, type `call` (a transcript), `document` or `email`, key the stem, with exactly these body lines: `path: <record-relative path>`, `sha256: <hex of the saved bytes>`, `input_kind: <transcript|document|email> · fixture: <yes|no> · asked_by: <short id> · redactions: <n>` and, for an attachment only, `came_with: <record-relative path of the email input>`. A roll moves whole entries, verbatim, out of the record log: at a release every entry at the strand's position whose version is the released version moves into `versions/<v>/log.md`, and at a strand close every remaining entry at the strand's position moves into `strands/<id>/log.md`. Each rolled file is new, with front matter `schema: "log/1"`, `client`, `strand`, `version` (or `"none"`), `rolled_at`, `entries` and `sha256`, and each roll leaves one pointer entry at the strand position (type `release` with key `released`, or type `strand` with key `closed`) whose body is `rolled_to: <record-relative path>` and `entries: <n> · sha256: <hex>`. An entry's hash is the sha256 of its header and body lines with line endings normalised to LF, and the roll hash covers the moved entries in file order. `record.py check` blocks when a rolled file's recomputed hash differs from its front matter or its pointer entry, and, by the baseline `.work/history.json`, when an entry was changed or removed; it warns on an entry absent from the baseline and on a missing baseline, which the next insert rebuilds.

## Evidence markers and the citation contract

| Marker | Meaning | Travels with it | Counts toward the rubric |
|---|---|---|---|
| `[Q]` | client quote, verbatim, never tidied; intake `certain`, or `probable` with the qualifier travelling with it | speaker name and role, side, location | yes, client side only |
| `[Q?]` | quote with an unclear speaker (intake `uncertain`) | as `[Q]`, plus a note | Partial until the user confirms |
| `[Q/side]` | side-only quote, speaker not resolvable (intake `unattributable`, or a segment of a composite block) | the side, the unit or block range, a note | Partial, never Missing |
| `[Q/recon]` | a sentence assembled from several units after boundary de-duplication | as `[Q]`, plus every unit number | as the weakest unit it rests on |
| `[Q/doc]` | a verbatim extract from a client document (A16) | the document, its section or page, the author by role where known, the document's date and status | yes for current-state and requirements criteria; cost, why-now, decision-process and next-step only where stated explicitly |
| `[X]` | quarantined: spoken inside a demonstration frame that is ours with a screen shared, or under a hypothetical marker | the frame's location and the reason | never |
| `[E]` | external fact from a dated source | source key `S#` from the register | no |
| `[I]` | inference | its basis and a confidence word | no; it can only shape a question |
| `[A]` | assumption: a build decision made without evidence, from the readiness check's assumptions, including an assumed term (an automation condition or the board-architecture overview not yet confirmed) and a fork built on its leaning (A25) | its basis, the question that would replace it, and, once the user reports it, the client's confirmation or explicit acceptance, which clears it | no; it shapes the build and the next questions, stays assumed until cleared, and is never written into monday as if the client had said it |

Locations follow the file's own grammar: `@HH:MM:SS` (a file whose lines carry minutes and seconds alone is written with a zero hour, `@00:03:44`, the form the leak lint and `ledger.py` read; `record.py check` also reads `@MM:SS` as a location), `cue 82`, `cues 82-85` for a merged run, `L142` or `L142-144`, `paragraph 75`; a document extract cites `inputs/<stem>.md §3.2` or `p.4`. Markers live in `research/`, `calls/`, `inputs/`, `readiness.md` and the internal parts of the blueprint; `leak_lint.py` blocks all nine in `sop/`, `drafts/`, `deliverables/` and every monday payload. Four tiers: attributed (`[Q]`), attributed with a qualifier (`probable`, or `[Q?]`), side-only (`[Q/side]`), refuse; a `[Q/doc]` whose author is known is attributed, otherwise "the client's document", one step below. Refusal is mandatory for a sub-clause fragment, a re-joined sentence whose load-bearing half changed speaker, a quote resting on an assembled figure, a second-hand re-quote of another call, and any quote whose first or last word sits under another speaker's label — the speaker boundary, never the turn boundary.
