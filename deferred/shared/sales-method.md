# The sales method

The one home for the user's sales method inside the plugin: principles, the discovery readiness rubric, the call-type scope matrix, the client-document rules, the demo principles, the objection map, the short support-call instrument and the claims rules. Skills link here and never restate it. Section numbers mirror the method dossier the design cites as "method §n", so a citation such as §2.6, §5.2 or §7 resolves in this file. Every name a fixture key matches on — criterion, per-pain check, gate, verdict, objection type, support check — is spelled here exactly as the keys spell it. Record shapes and field names live in `record-map.md`; the voice lives in `house-style.md`; delivery-confidentiality rules live in `safety.md`; the platform never-say block lives beside the gotchas in `monday-gotchas.md`.

Sources: the method dossier as revised by the real-call calibration (`REAL-CALL-LESSONS.md`, cited as C-numbers; its §2 and §7 are the source of the scope matrix in 2.6), the architecture (A§3.3, A§7.5, A§7.6, A§8.3, A§8.4) and the amendments A13, A15, A16 and A17. Every rule resting on one observation is marked provisional and ships as a hypothesis with the observation that would confirm it. Nothing here carries content from a real call: every example is generic.

## 1. Core principles

1. **Diagnose before prescribing.** No demo before discovery, no price before a bleed number, no proposal before the client has agreed the scope out loud.
2. **The client's words make the case.** Their statements and numbers outweigh anything the user or the plugin asserts. Quotes are recorded verbatim, never tidied.
3. **Ask, listen, stay with the problem.** In discovery the client does most of the talking (target 60% or more). When they describe a pain, explore it before offering a fix. This holds while the client walks their current state; when they walk a candidate solution they built themselves, interrogating it hard is the work and silence is the defect (2.5).
4. **Put a price on doing nothing.** Work out the bleed together; where it fits, also the upside.
5. **Everything shown or built traces to a stated pain.** Anything else goes to the backlog, not the demo.
6. **A demo is a conversation.** Short showings, a check-in after each, stop once they are convinced, end with a commitment question rather than a summary. The close is a separate discipline that good delivery does not produce on its own (4.6).
7. **Most objections are a skipped step.** Prevent them by running the step early; when one surfaces anyway, stay curious and never defensive.
8. **Trusted expert, not order taker.** Guide the conversation, and say so when the current setup only needs a small fix.
9. **Every meeting ends with a next step the client assented to.** A date, time and owner where the room can fix one; in expansion mode or under the working-session sub-trigger, a booking mechanism with a named owner and a stated purpose counts (2.3, R10). A promise to send something over is neither. Every support call ends with one forward-looking question about what is still manual.
10. **Internal sales thinking never reaches client-facing material** — or a shared screen (7).
11. **What is said aloud is held to the rule for what is written.** No figure without a source, no capability without a probe, no estimate for work nobody has read, and a correction made in the next meeting for anything said wrong (7.1).

## 2. Discovery readiness rubric

### 2.1 Grading rules

- **Input.** Every call the strand serves, any client document taken in (2.7), and the previous `discovery.md`. `research/` supplies context only. A package handed over at once is graded once, as a set, after the whole set is in the record (A15); one call alone is the common case and nothing waits for a second. Each run records which calls and documents it covered, by sequence rather than file name.
- **Scope.** Graded per pain (2.2), for the engagement (2.3), and per typed segment where a call changed type part-way (2.6). Two parallel discovery threads on one account — two sponsors, two pieces of work — are graded per thread, each thread a strand, shown side by side and never averaged.
- **Status values.** Written in the record exactly as the second column spells them. The reviewed fixtures s1 to s10 carry only `met`, `partial` and `missing`; the four calibration values below appear in no reviewed key, and the grades of s1 to s10 do not change; `met-with-residual`, `not-obtainable` and the `asked` flag are carried by the calibration keys s11 to s25, and `advisory` by none of them, because every fixture's type resolves: its positive case is the constructed unresolvable fragment of BUILD-PLAN.md section 4.3, item 8 (H50), and s25 asserts its absence for a document-only record.

| Status | Written as | Meaning | Counts in the verdict and the gates as |
|---|---|---|---|
| Met | `met` | At least one client-side verbatim quote, or an explicit document extract (2.7), satisfies the definition | met |
| Met-with-residual | `met-with-residual` | A compound criterion whose elements are all Met but one, where the definition is Met without that element; the missing element becomes a gap question instead of vanishing into a one-word grade. Every compound criterion is graded element by element, the elements shown, the weakest reported | met; the residual is a question, never a demotion |
| Partial | `partial` | Present but short of the definition: vague, an unconfirmed range, a figure our side proposed that the client did not clearly confirm, speaker unclear, side-only evidence | partial |
| Missing | `missing` | Nothing in the inputs | missing |
| Not obtainable from this room | `not-obtainable` | Nothing in the inputs, and the role that could supply it was not present. Reported as "not obtainable from this room" with that role named from the obtainability map (2.3) | missing; the prep routes to a room change, not to the same question again |
| Advisory | `advisory` | A type-specific rule demoted because the call's type could not be resolved (2.6). Carries its question; regraded once the user confirms the type | excluded from the verdict and from every gate |
| Not applicable | `not-applicable` | Set only by the user, with a reason that goes to `log.md` | excluded |

- **The `asked` flag.** Every criterion and every per-pain check records `asked` (yes or no) with the attempted question quoted where one exists. "We never asked" and "they would not say" grade the same and need different prep: a dodged item needs a different room, person or framing, never the same question twice. Cost inputs and Trajectory are ask-only: volunteered when the person who does the work is in the room, otherwise obtained only by asking.
- **Evidence format.** Quote, speaker (name, role, side), the check or element it satisfies, file, location, marker, intake confidence — the eight markers and the citation contract are A§7.5, pinned in `record-map.md`. The worst confidence state travels to any pain, number or requirement derived from the quote. Material the client sent before a call is cited `[pre-read]` with who sent it and whether our side acknowledged it on the call (provisional, one observation).
- **What can make an item Met.** Only `[found]` client-side evidence, or a client document that states the thing explicitly (2.7). `[inferred]` items are shown, count as Missing for gates, and can only shape a question. Side-only evidence — "a participant on the client side" — grades Partial and never Missing. A figure our side supplied counts only through the client's explicit confirming quote.
- **Non-evidence.** None of the following counts, for any criterion: our own framing and recaps; backchannel tokens ("right", "mm"); agreement that answers a meeting proposal rather than a substantive claim; a feature request phrased as a question; any date or figure our side supplied, including a figure elicited by our own leading band ("it shouldn't be more than that, though?"), which is our figure and not a client input; a client figure re-quoted second-hand by a seller who handed the package over (A15); and a client assent swallowed inside our own turn, which is permanently unprovable and is re-asked in the next call, naming the person. For R11 and R12 a backchannel counts as nothing rather than Partial, and the evidence quote is paired with the line it answers so an agreement cannot float free of its antecedent. **Two carve-outs, stated here so no grader has to find them.** First: a closed factual yes or no answering our own leading question is evidence *of the fact* but not *of their framing*, and is recorded with the question it answers. Second: R10 is asymmetric — the date or mechanism we proposed and the client assented to is the one admissible piece of our-side evidence there is; read literally, the list would delete the only next-step evidence that exists. (C14)
- **Several client voices.** A pain may be excavated across speakers — one states it, another prices the effort, a third quantifies the workaround's failure — provided they work in the same process, with the speaker recorded per check rather than per pain. Where a criterion is about behaviour or attitude, quote the whole turn. A proposition about one client speaker's work, supplied by a different client speaker and confirmed only with a token, is capped at Partial and records both speakers (provisional). (C19)
- **No or unreliable speaker labels.** Mark evidence "speaker unclear" and grade Partial until the user confirms. Grading is per turn, not per file: one repaired block caps its own evidence at Partial and never downgrades the whole transcript. Zero artifact markers together with shred or composite signals raises the doubt level rather than lowering it.
- **Conflicts** are section 3.

### 2.2 Per-pain checks (run for every pain P#)

| Check | Met when the client... | If missing or partial, add to the next prep (example phrasing) |
|---|---|---|
| Stated | describes the problem in their own words | (defines the pain; no P# without it) |
| Concrete | gives a specific recent occurrence, or describes what it looks like day to day (what, who, how often) | "When did that last come up? What happened?" |
| Workaround | names what they do today and why carrying on that way is not acceptable | "What stops you from carrying on with the current workaround?" |
| Reach | says who else it affects and how | "Who else ends up dealing with this?" |
| Cost inputs | (ask-only) attaches hours, headcount, frequency, errors or lost revenue | "Across everyone involved, roughly how many hours a week go into this?" — to the person who does the work |
| Trajectory | (ask-only) says what happens to it in a year, or as they grow, if nothing changes | "Say this stays as it is. What does next year look like?" |

A pain with Stated, Concrete and Workaround all Met is **excavated**; the three may come from different client speakers in the same process. The full sequence runs on every pain, but only one excavated pain is required for R2. The six check names are exactly `Stated`, `Concrete`, `Workaround`, `Reach`, `Cost inputs`, `Trajectory`; rows are written `P1 Stated`, `P1 Concrete`, `P1 Workaround`, `P1 Reach`, `P1 Cost inputs`, `P1 Trajectory`, one row per check per graded pain.

A figure the client scopes to the pain in the same clause needs no separate attribution quote where the referent is explicit. Where the referent is a pointer — "that", "this one", "the thing we showed you" — record what it resolves to and how, and the check cannot exceed Partial until confirmed with the person who performs the work; otherwise the bleed is built on the cost of doing the work rather than the cost of the pain. A total-effort or total-spend figure is never a cost input unless the client attributes a portion of it to the pain, and that quote is required. Cost inputs arrive in proportion to how narrow and countable the unit of work is: with the operator present on a working session they arrive as a by-product of the scoping argument, with no cost question asked. (C16, C21)

### 2.3 Engagement criteria

The criterion column is the exact string the keys and the record use, `R1 Current state mapped` through `R12 Scope agreed verbally`. "Compound" marks a criterion graded element by element, where `met-with-residual` can apply.

| Criterion | Met when | Evidence to record | If missing or partial | Weight |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| R1 Current state mapped | Compound. The client has described the process end to end; the tools and how they connect or do not; who touches it; time spent on their own process and time spent maintaining the platform (two elements, Met on either, the record showing which); what monday does today and who built it | Quotes per element; `[pre-read]` material and client documents can satisfy elements; where the client showed us their build, the structure inventory with observed-only rows recorded as unconfirmed; a requirement re-engineered by consensus on a call keeps its mechanism history | Walkthrough question for the unmapped element; the blueprint lists assumptions to confirm | Gate: blueprint |
| R2 Pain made concrete | At least one pain is excavated (2.2) | Pain ids and quotes, speaker per check | Per-pain questions for the top pain | Core |
| R3 Why now | Compound. Three client-side elements — what changed, a trigger event, a clock. Met on change plus at least one other. A date our side supplied and a trigger event our side initiated (our own outreach) are excluded, or a prospect who merely answered a call would grade Met with no urgency at all | Quote per element, dates. Our own commercial clock — a renewal, a quarter end, any constraint on our side — is recorded and front-paged separately as an owned to-do, never as client hesitancy, and excluded from the grade (C15) | "What's made this matter now rather than last year?" Anticipate timing and think-it-over objections | Core |
| R4 Bleed number | Compound. Yearly (or monthly) cost of the current state built from client-supplied inputs, math written out, total confirmed by the client. | R5 Upside number | Freed capacity converted to value with the client's figures, and the client names something they want to do but cannot today | Quotes, math | Where it fits: "If that time went into selling, what would it add up to in a month?" then "Is there something you'd go after if capacity weren't the limit?" | Advisory |
| R6 Desired outcome | Compound. The client describes what solved looks like day to day, and what their leadership would count as a win | Quotes, turned into draft success criteria | "Imagine this is running well by spring. What's different in a normal week?" The blueprint marks success criteria as proposed | Gate: v1.0 |
| R7 Decision process mapped | Compound. Who is involved, their roles, what each cares about, who approves spend, what happens after a yes, prior budgeting experience — recorded as a delegation graph where the room described one: delegate, gate-holder, gate condition, re-entry trigger, standing conditions. "Run it, but I want to be in on the approvals" is one sentence and the whole decision structure of an engagement, and a flat role list cannot hold it (C77) | Per-stakeholder quotes; the graph | "Who else has a say, and what will they want to see?" Recommend inviting the approver; anticipate the check-with-someone objection | Core |
| R8 Priority and timing | The client places it on a now-versus-later scale and names a target date or real deadline | Quote | "Is this something to fix this quarter, or later on?" A "later" answer is flagged to the user as a fit risk | Advisory |
| R9 Blockers surfaced | The client has said what could stop this going ahead | Quote | "What could get in the way of this going ahead?" near the close of the next meeting | Advisory |
| R10 Next step committed | The call ended with a specific date, time and owner. In expansion mode or under the working-session sub-trigger (2.6): a date, **or** a booking mechanism delivered during the call with a named owner, a stated purpose and the client's assent; scheduling handed off to a named owner without that assent is `partial` with a same-day date-proposal to-do (the method's "Partial-plus" is that pairing, not a further status); an offer to find some times is neither. Nothing is keyed on post-call calendar state, which a transcript cannot show (C26) | Quote — the one criterion where our own proposal, assented to, is admissible (2.1) | Flag in the call summary; the recap draft proposes a concrete time; unowned commitments flagged | Every call |

| R12 Scope agreed verbally | The client agreed a short scope (five bullets or fewer) out loud. Where the client built part of the thing themselves, the scope is a delta in four parts — inherited, in scope, client-side change, access unlock — each confirmed with the person who owns it; without that, both sides price different objects while believing they agree (C113) | Quote, paired with the line it answers | No proposal draft; a scope-confirmation step added to the next prep | Gate: proposal |

**On R10.** Across the calibration's evidence no new date was fixed during any call; what separated a good close from a bad one was a mechanism with a named owner. The date rule stays for discovery and demo calls, because a date in the room is attainable there, the close (4.6) is written to obtain it, and fixture s2 grades "I'll send something over" as `missing`; the mechanism state applies only in the two modes named above. The written rule wins on grading; the observed practice wins on how the close is built.

**Which role can supply what.** A Missing grade is only actionable once it is known who could have answered. This map turns `missing` into `not-obtainable` and routes it to a room change; each gap question (2.5) names the role. (C74)

| Criterion or check | Usually supplied by |
|---|---|
| Current state, workaround, concrete instance | the operator who does the work; the admin or builder for what the platform does today |
| Cost inputs, effort per unit | the operator who does the work (volunteered when present); the department head for the aggregate |
| Volume, headcount, frequency | the department head |
| Why now, the clock, priority | the executive or budget holder |
| Decision process, blockers, procurement | the gate-holder, in their own words |
| Access, policy, security questions | the client's IT or security owner — never answered by us |
| Scope of a client-built artefact | its author |

**Expansion and renewal mode.** One mode, not two. It applies when the relationship is an existing client of ours or a live build already exists, and it carries a **working-session sub-trigger** — technical work performed in the meeting, or a client-built artefact present — rather than a second mode. In it current state is expected Met early; why-now, bleed and decision process are the scarce criteria the prep targets (the decision-process claim is provisional and confounded with room composition). Three record fields: *current wins to preserve* (quote, object, owner, authorship — built by us, by them or by a third party — the burden of keeping it and its change path); *prior attempts* (what was built, by whom, when, its status and who paid, in their words, presuming neither failure nor lapse); *incumbent friction* (anything the client has said about the cost or difficulty of changing what we previously built, recorded either way including "nothing said"). An advisory item documents what already works. What we built and is still live lives at record level in `prior_builds` and the live map, not here. (C23)

**Client-built assets and inherited-build risk.** Where the client built something themselves, the record holds it in its own section with the blocker in their own words, which is usually the actual scope rather than the pain list. Before any commitment: authorship, whether we can read it, the cost of discarding it, who owns it after handover. No completion, effort or duration figure is quoted for work on a client-built artefact until we can read it (7.1). A separately priced review step is a recommendation to the user, never a rule the product enforces. (C111, C112, provisional)

**Bleed and upside math rules.** Bleed: hours per week × loaded hourly cost × 52 (or monthly × 12), per pain, then summed; errors, rework and lost deals are added only when the client gives a figure, otherwise listed as unquantified. Upside: freed hours, then extra output × the client's average value, then the "what would you do with it" question; upside is never added to the cost of inaction, and loaded cost stays apart from bill rates. Use the client's figures; ranges stay ranges; never round up; never fill a missing input with an outside average; every estimate of ours is `[I]` and kept apart. A figure whose unit was abandoned mid-sentence is stored *unit-unresolved*, neither a range nor a hedge. A hedged number keeps its hedge. A figure that entered the call from outside it — a colleague's message, a lookup mid-call — is off-transcript and never promoted to confirmed. Between two figures whose relationship is unresolved (3) no arithmetic runs. A bleed smaller than the likely engagement cost is flagged to the user as a fit question, not a pricing problem. **Platform consumption is a design input, not a bleed input and not pricing**: where any component the solution will use is metered — which under A17 includes AI agents, AI columns and Vibe apps — record the allowance held, what already consumes it and by what, whether charging is per run or per publication, expiry and reset behaviour, and a measurement plan, in its own `discovery.md` section cross-linked to the bleed frame (C103; where it sits relative to the frame is open, L15).

### 2.4 Verdict and stage gates

**Verdict**, written exactly so: **Discovery complete** when R2, R3, R4 and R7 are all Met (`met` or `met-with-residual`); **Nearly ready** when none of those is Missing and at least one is Partial; **Not ready** when any of those is Missing (`missing` or `not-obtainable`). An `advisory` grade counts for nothing in the verdict. The verdict and a one-line reason go to `discovery.md`, `STRAND.md` and the strand index; the reason names the missing multiplier where scale is established and cost is not priced, any open question blocking a gate with its owner, and a commercial-date collision.

**Readiness section, beyond the grades.** A *voices heard / voices missing* line built from the roster against who spoke; where no roster exists it reads "unknown", never "none". Attendees who never spoke and hold a decision-shaped role are promoted to a named risk row recording whether our side ever addressed them. Criteria met only by the people who already like the tool are discounted in the reason line. The `asked` flags sit beside the grades. (C20)

**Stage gates**, named exactly as the keys name them. Build and First demo count as met only when the blueprint gate is met, since they add no method requirement of their own.

| Gate | Needs | If not met |
|---|---|---|
| Solution blueprint | R1 at least Partial, R2 Met; where a structure inventory exists (2.6), its graded read-back | Explain the gap, offer a discovery prep; the user may override |
| Build | The user's approval at the existing approval stop | (no extra method gate) |
| First demo (v0.1) | Nothing extra; the version audited | Gaps become demo questions |
| Pricing or proposal prep | For a figure: R4, R7 and R12 Met. Disclosing that a component is consumption-priced is not gated (4.10). Pricing and proposal prep stay outside the product: an open gate is reported as open and handed to the user, never answered with a figure or a document | Prepare the missing step instead |
| v1.0 sign-off | R6 success criteria agreed; R11 Met for every in-scope pain, with mechanism-confirmed pains re-confirmed on screen | Add re-checks to the sign-off demo |

A strand whose deliverable is outside monday (kind `non-platform`, or the non-platform half of a `hybrid` one — A13 as narrowed by A17, so AI agents, AI columns and Vibe apps are platform work and never this path) has no blueprint, build, demo or audit gate. Its path is the **scope record and hand-off**: scope, open questions, dependencies, access needed and next steps recorded, ending with the fixed hand-off sentence `record-map.md` pins, and the plain statement that building happens outside the product. The gates the product owns still apply to what it writes: claims discipline, the leak lint, and the approval stop on anything written into the account.

**A commercial date against a closed gate.** When a commercial date exists — on the client's side or ours — and the pricing gate is closed, schedule the missing steps backwards from that date and show the collision on the front page rather than waiting for an override nobody wants to give (C25). **Overrides** are the user's call: the override and its reason go to `log.md` and the gap stays visible in `STRAND.md`.

### 2.5 Turning gaps into meeting prep

- Each Missing or Partial item yields one short, conversational question tied to its P# or R#, stored in the strand's open-questions register and pulled into the next prep, with the person who can answer it (2.3). Show what is already known beside each question; remove questions once answered.
- At most five gap questions per meeting, counted per audience — the people who will actually be in the room — and per kind (conflict, technical, access, commercial, policy, ask). Order: core criteria, then gates for the upcoming step, then advisory items. In a demo, attach each to the segment it follows naturally (4.4).
- When a gap cannot be closed by asking — the approver never attends, the role that could answer was not in the room — propose an action instead: invite them, or offer to join their internal conversation. A question asked and dodged is not repeated as written; it goes to a different person, room or framing.
- **Discovery prep order:** brief connection question and let them talk first; set the frame that this is a conversation; honest-expert line where it fits ("If a small fix is all you need, I'll tell you."); current state; per pain (concrete, workaround, reach); why now; cost and confirmation; upside; outcome; decision, priority, blockers; commitment question and a specific next step.
- **Group prep order:** let the senior person set the frame; take each role in turn, by name, with the reason they own the question; then have them demonstrate. List attendees with a stated stake and no contribution, ranked by stake; at least one gets a direct question by name before the close and the rest go to the convener to route. Never every silent attendee put on the spot. (C29)
- **When the client is showing.** Two disciplines, and the difference is *what* they show. Walking their **current state** (a reverse demo): stop selling while they are showing; questions go to the inventory — what is this, who owns it, where does the value come from — and nothing else, because a fix proposed across their narration flattens the requirement about to be said. Walking a **candidate solution they built** (a prototype review): sustained interrogation of the artefact *is* the work and silence is the defect — probe the data source, the formats, the identifiers, the failure cases, what breaks it. Interrogating the artefact is not selling; what the rule targets is our side proposing a solution across their narration. The delivery-note check for a fix offered mid-pain fires only on the current-state case. Either way, ask for the shape of a thing rather than choosing an item for them to open (`safety.md`). (C29)

### 2.6 Call type, and what it switches

A call's type is four dimensions, each of which changes which criteria apply and at what threshold — the vocabulary is A§8.3 step 5, pinned in `record-map.md` and only cited here: **seat** (partner-led, co-sell, vendor-led, client-led); **relationship** (new logo, existing client of ours, a third party's account where we are the stranger); **direction of show** (we show; they show their current state; they show a candidate solution they built; nobody shows); **output class** (configured structure, bespoke code, hybrid — a hybrid is two work items with two gate sets, never a blend). Two **modifiers** change thresholds without changing structure: room shape, and whether money is live in the room. Intake proposes the type as a header on the call summary with the quote that produced it and never decides it; the user dismisses or confirms. (C105)

- **Per segment.** A call is not one type throughout: when a screen stops, client share and the rate of substantive client turns jump, and everything decision-relevant may arrive after that boundary. The header carries the primary type plus the segment boundaries with the event at each, and grading, evidence harvesting and delivery notes run per segment: the last third of a demo that turned into discovery is harvested as discovery. (C107)
- **Unresolved type.** Where a dimension cannot be resolved, the header names its candidates, the conservative fallback per dimension applies — the value that grants us the fewest rights and demands the most evidence: seat, not ours to control; relationship, new logo; direction of show, no screen; output class, bespoke, so nothing may be claimed buildable; commercial, live with the approver absent — and every criterion with a specified cell on that dimension drops to `advisory`: it may raise a question but never produces a Missing verdict or a failed gate. The demotion stops at the rubric. The strand's `kind` and `output_class`, which the user sets at strand open, still govern the build, audit and demo doors, so nothing is claimed buildable on an unresolved class and no door goes advisory (A§12.3 D26). A client document has no type at all (2.7).
- **Reverse demo and prototype review** produce the **structure inventory**: boards, groups, columns, statuses, views and dashboards, each row marked *spoken*, *observed* or *documented*, each with an owner and a class (current state, current manual artefact, client-built prototype), so current state can be satisfied without the prototype rows; two further states, *proposed-and-displaced* with the displacing quote, and *compound show*. Its graded read-back — the current-state analogue of R11 — sits in front of the blueprint gate. A prototype review additionally records the inheritance: which tool, what works, the named gap in their words, who owns the code, what access we need, what must not break, their own assessment of completeness (provisional: the type switches no rule off on its own until a second observation arrives, C110). Fork detection does not live in the inventory; it belongs wherever client speakers narrate their current state, in any call type. (C22)
- **Scoping and working-session calls** are routed, not dropped, and not graded first: open questions with owners, the dependency chain and the strand kind the call implies are recorded before anything else, so the next step is the scope path and never a blueprint or a door. Grading, when the user asks for it, runs under expansion mode's working-session sub-trigger (2.3). A bespoke-code strand has no structure read-back, no automation list and no demo; "the thing cannot be demonstrated yet" is a stated stop. (C109, C114, A13)
- **A seat we do not own.** Next step and scope are graded against the thread rather than against us, and commercial answers have one named owner (4.11). (C108)

**The scope matrix.** Source: `REAL-CALL-LESSONS.md` §2 and §7, with method §2.6. Rows are the dimension values and the two modifiers; columns are the criteria. A cell is one of three values. **Met-threshold**: the calibration evidenced a type-specific grading rule for this cell; the numbered note gives the threshold. **advisory**: the calibration evidenced that the criterion is graded but excluded from the verdict and the gates on this value. **unspecified**: the calibration did not evidence a type-specific rule here, so the plain definition of 2.2 and 2.3 applies unchanged — which is how s1 to s10 are graded — and nothing is invented; `unspecified` never means the criterion is skipped. ### 2.7 Client documents as evidence (A16)

A document is authored, not spoken. Its author, date and status (draft, approved, current practice, desired state) are recorded and each left `unknown` where the document does not say; it is cited `[Q/doc]` by document and section or page, never by speaker, at the attributed tier where the author is known and one step below — "the client's document" — where not. A process document feeds current state; a requirements or "what we want" write-up feeds the requirement trace and never the current-state picture; one document can carry both, section by section. A flowchart or process map is read from the image or PDF into steps and decision points in the structure inventory under the *documented* marker. **What a document can satisfy:** R1 and the current-state elements, R6's desired-outcome picture and R12's scope where it states them, and any requirements criterion. **What it cannot satisfy unless it states the thing in so many words:** R3, R4, R7 and R10 — cost, why-now, decision process and next step; a figure it states is a client figure with the document as its source. A document-only record still gets a grade: no type header is proposed, nothing drops to `advisory` for a header it does not carry, every one of R1 to R12 is graded against its plain definition, and every gap the document cannot fill is shown as a gap. Beside a call, the call's header scopes the criteria and the document's extracts are admitted under these same rules. Documented process is not lived process: where a document and a call disagree, both are kept (3).

## 3. Conflicts, forks and superseded figures

**The narrowed rule.** The most recent client statement wins only for a factual conflict about the **same proposition, from the same speaker, inside one call**; then the superseded value and date go to `log.md` as a `superseded` entry. Every other pair is a **conflict** kept as both statements with call, speaker and location — or document and section — in the strand's `open-questions.md` with kind `conflict`, marked unresolved, and raised as a gap question routed to the person who can settle it: two speakers on one call; the same speaker giving two accounts of one fact, restricted to assertion classes a follow-up message would repeat back (who attended a prior meeting, a plan or tier, a figure stated two ways, an owner named two ways) and never time-of-day remarks, which surface transcription noise; two calls, where no recency rule applies (A15); and a document against a call, which is a finding in its own right and often the best callback material there is (A16). Two figures minutes apart that may price different line items are recorded as a pair with the relationship unresolved and no arithmetic between them. **A structural disagreement is a fork, not a conflict**: two speakers who agree on the facts but need different structures are carried as an open option in the blueprint, and recency must not resolve it (C91). A requirement re-engineered by consensus inside one call is not a conflict either; its mechanism history is kept. (C19, A§3.3, A§7.6)

## 4. Demo principles

Full run-sheet mechanics are the `demo-script` skill's; this section is the method it follows. A run-sheet is gated on **we show**. Where the client shows or nobody shows, the artifact is a scoping run-sheet — the artefact to be walked, the dependency questions in order, the mechanism proposals to be named, a scope read-back — and before any mechanism is proposed, where each input lives today, in what format and who puts it there. A strand with nothing built inside the product cannot be demonstrated, and the generator stops with that statement (A13).

### 4.1 Prep essentials
One goal for the meeting. Attendees with decision role and audience type, both sides' hard stops, and whether the approver attends. A duration the client stated in their words, converted to a number and checked. Two or three callbacks in verbatim client words, second-hand ones marked. The stated buying criteria, verbatim. Numbers sourced (bleed and upside spoken only if it fits, never on screen), every other figure read live at prep with its date or user-approved, and the **numbers we do not have** card — one line per unsourced category with the sentence to say instead and the person who can source it. Each capability on the **shown / buildable / unverified** ladder; only the first two are stated as capability, and the account-and-plan difference is said aloud only where the capability is plan-gated or the client stated a plan constraint. Artefacts to request before the call, because a question whose answer could be read off a document is an artefact request and not a demo question. Gap questions capped per audience (2.5). At most three likely objections (5.3). The environment check and provenance line per screen (`safety.md`). The corrections from last meeting (7.1). The planned close (4.6).

### 4.2 Opening (one to three minutes)
Greet, a short connection question, let them talk first; open with who you are, who is with you and why, and what the pre-read told you. Three sentences of frame: each piece shown links to something they said and interruptions are welcome; the plan boundary; the seeded-data boundary. Check priorities with a question, not a recap. Where their structure and the proposed one differ, a two-minute translation beat reads the structure mapping back in their words, and where two shapes are possible offers both aloud and lets them choose. Define the platform once, plainly, for a first-exposure room. An agenda fork is a second-meeting move, offered only where every branch has a prepared run-sheet.

### 4.3 Segment pattern (one per pain)
Callback in their words, one per segment, never one aggregated paraphrase; show, under two minutes, only what serves this pain; value in their vocabulary; the **named** check-in to the person whose pain it is — the only check-in recordable as a confirmation; listen for yes signals, gaps, objections and the client tying the screen to their own pain, which the next presenter sentence confirms in their words and logs in the **per-pain confirmation ledger** (capped at ties that map to a named pain, so an interactive stretch never becomes a parroting loop); optionally one gap question. A **room-wide open** check-in — one per phase boundary, naming the topic, tolerating silence, preferably from whoever is not driving — is for discovering a stake the record does not hold, permitted only where the room holds such attendees, and never a confirmation; a closed comprehension check may open the floor and is never evidence (C36). Order: the pain tied to why now first, then the client's emphasis. Never more planned segments than stated pains; showings generated live in answer to a question are exempt and counted separately. About ninety seconds of our side without a client word, counted across both presenters with a one-turn hand-off exemption, means the next line is a routed question. An ask repeated across meetings and people is a build item, not a talking point, including one answered in words every time; the run-sheet refuses to leave the most-repeated ask unbuilt. A first-exposure audience gets the orientation pattern — name the primitive, show one instance, state what composes on it, confirm comprehension, route a discovery question (provisional). Where the portfolio default applies, a job-level segment goes to the person who works at that level.

### 4.4 Discovery during the demo
The demo continues discovery. The cap is on questions whose answers cannot change the next two minutes, not on the count (provisional as a general licence: a single presenter who stops ten times has nobody holding the floor). Current-state data questions come before any screen that presupposes data. Rubric gaps first; then standing questions such as "Who would have this open every morning?" and "Which part of the week would feel different first?" Answers update `discovery.md`.

### 4.5 Exit ramps and hesitation, as literal sentences
Buying signals: explicit confirmation, questions about timing, cost, rollout or next steps, future-tense use, asking who else should see it, and the strongest — the client building their own scenario in the tool, unprompted, which ends the showing. Action: finish the point, skip the remaining segments to "if asked", go to the close. The urge to say "it can also..." is the cue for the commitment question. Hesitation — quiet or vague answers, three or more hedges in one turn — means stop and add nothing. An explicit redirect outranks every hedge and ends our elaboration on the next sentence, but not a client-initiated thread; overrun is measured from the end of that thread. A client vouching for an unshown capability sourced to a meeting we were not on is a buying signal and an unverified claim at once; the next sentence confirms the scope or qualifies it and the claim goes to the correction list (7.1).

### 4.6 Close (two to four minutes)
The close is generated in full because delivery quality does not produce it: the **commitment question** first ("From what you've seen, does this deal with the problem you described?" then "Where would you like to go from here?"), never an opinion question; **per-pain confirmation** from the person who raised each pain, read back from the ledger rather than asked cold; the **direction question**; the spoken **gap-and-backlog line** from `gaps.md`, naming what was asked for and not shown; "What could get in the way?" if hesitation is in the air; and the **next step as a mechanism**, the invite sent on the call — where the room cannot fix a date, scheduling handed to a named owner through a named mechanism with the client's assent (R10 still needs the date on a discovery or demo call). Never read the room: no nonverbal read, enthusiasm read or self-assessment of fit stands in for the commitment question. A proposal is offered only after R12. A scoping session closes with a scope read-back in five bullets, each confirmed with its owner, then the paid-next-step question, then the booking mechanism. Whoever controls the screen stays to the close, so the meeting ends before either side's hard stop. (C40)

### 4.7 Timing variants (roughly three to four minutes per segment)
Ten minutes: open 1, segments 7, close 2, one or two pains, at most one gap question. Twenty: open 2, segments 14, close 3-4, three or four pains. Thirty: open 3, segments 17, open conversation 5, close 4-5, four or five pains. Cut segments rather than rush a showing; a long open-question phase is protected **before** the close and never lengthens it, and a run-sheet that protects one flags the tension rather than overriding it quietly.

### 4.8 Audience variants
Executives get pains tied to why now, dashboards and outcomes; admins get structure, automations and how to change things; end users get their own view and one real task end to end. Mixed audience: approver's segment first, one segment per role, a named check-in after each.

### 4.9 Version variants
v0.1 confirms direction; v0.x callbacks point to the last feedback and the correction list is read first; v1.0 is one segment per in-scope pain against agreed criteria with R11 confirmation per pain; v1.x callbacks come from backlog seeds.

### 4.10 Pricing conversation guard (internal; only when the user asks)
Two halves, gated differently. Disclosing that a component is *consumption-priced* is a risk disclosure — allowed early and mandatory wherever a component is metered (C98). Naming a *figure* needs R4, R7 and R12 Met; otherwise prepare the missing step. Prep order for a figure: their bleed in their words; what solving it is worth in their words; scope in five bullets or fewer; the price once; a pause; a calm comparison with their bleed if they react; the commitment question. No unprompted discounts, no justifying the rate, no filling the silence. **Never a leading price question** whose answer would later be quoted as a client input, and never reasoning from one vendor's pricing to another's (C99, provisional). **Audience and role:** no price, rate, entitlement or account-status figure after the person who owns approval has left the call, and none in a group-facing recap or a summary addressed to a mixed audience; the product never makes a non-approver the messenger for a figure, and one spoken anyway becomes an owned, dated to-do addressed to the approver, raised in the summary. Trigger: only where a gate-holder has been identified and was present earlier in the same call. The user may override it and the override is recorded. This rule lives here once; the rubric and the summary rules apply it from here (C97, provisional). The plugin holds no rate; figures come from the user, and every plan-limit, quota and entitlement figure is read live at prep with its date and kept in the claims store outside the record (`safety.md`).

### 4.11 Group calls and the co-sell seat
Rooms of nine or more with several silent attendees are ordinary. Know who is there: a participant header is claimed, not observed. Route every question by name with the reason they own it; ask the same architecture question twice, once of whoever knows what is possible and once of whoever knows what is needed. Two check-ins (4.3). New attendees evict the planned meeting type unless prep protects it — an opening contract, a budgeted orientation block, or a split meeting (C79, provisional). Commercial statements are routed by who is in the room (4.10). **Co-sell** — a third party fronts and the user drives: the prep carries the driver and fronter split (who opens, drives, closes, answers commercial questions) and the hand-back sentence; one named person owns each topic, the non-owner routes rather than answers, and two people never answer one question at once. Every claim the other party makes in our presence is recorded verbatim, attributed and dated; any statement they make about our own services or scope is reclaimed as ours; routing a question to them does not make the answer sourced (C108). A client asking why some group is not in the room is an objection signal (5.3). The close reads no room.

## 5. Objection handling

### 5.1 Generic pattern
Pause, calm and curious. Ask what they mean, using their exact words. Find the real concern under the stated one: cost, timing, confidence, someone else's view, trust. Run the skipped step now (5.2). Let them propose the resolution ("What would you need to see to feel comfortable with that?"). Record their words, the real concern, what resolved it and the status. Responses never include invented claims (7). An objection raised on two or more calls is *recurring* and gets a prepared response. A concern restated three or more times inside a minute by one speaker, each restatement stronger, with only agreement tokens from our side between, is a **restated-and-unanswered** objection, raised with all three quotes (C80, provisional). An objection surfaced as a fact about today by the person who lives it is recorded as *embedded* with a client-attributed cause, not as a deal objection (C24). Objections and responses are internal-only.

### 5.2 Skipped-step map
The first column is the exact `type` string the keys use, optionally followed by a parenthetical nuance; an objection with no home here is typed `Outside the map: ` plus a short description.

| Objection type | Usually means this was skipped | Prevent with | If it surfaces anyway |
|---|---|---|---|
| Price feels high | R4 bleed | Cost questions before any price | Compare with their bleed; if there is none, offer to work it out together now |
| Needs to think it over | R3 why now, pain trajectory | Urgency and trajectory questions | "Sure. Which part are you still weighing?" then address that part |
| Must check with someone | R7 decision process | Decision questions; invite the approver | Ask what that person will care about most; offer to join that conversation |
| Wrong time, next quarter | R3, R8 | Why-now and priority questions | "What will be different next quarter?"; cost of waiting from their monthly bleed; tie to their real deadline |
| We'll build it ourselves | Workaround check (they maintain the current setup) | Workaround questions | "What has held it up so far, and whose time would it use?" — but where they built most of it and named the wall, nothing was skipped: that is the strongest buying signal there is, with the ask already scoped by them; credit the work, name the missing piece precisely, scope as a delta (R12), quote no figure until the artefact has been read (7.1) |
| Talking to other partners | R6 outcome, buying criteria | Outcome questions | Welcome it; "What have you seen so far, and what would make the choice clear?" |
| Just send a proposal or pricing | R12 scope | Scope confirmation before offering a document | "Happy to. Let's agree what it covers first, so the numbers mean something." |
| monday itself is the problem | R1 current state | Current-state walkthrough | Ask what it fails to do; offer to check whether the setup or the tool is the cause before a costly switch |
| Team won't adopt it | End users not heard | End-user conversation, end-user demo segment | Ask who would resist and why; plan an end-user session. Never a maintenance promise: the answer is running the skipped step, not a claim about upkeep (C47) |

### 5.3 Anticipating objections from the record
Before each prep, scan `research/`, `calls/` and `discovery.md`; list at most three likely objections with status *anticipated*: the signal (quote and source, or `[I]` and basis), the skipped step, prevention questions merged into the gap questions, and the response approach. Signals: R4 Missing or a small bleed or a budget freeze → price; R3 Missing, priority "later", no deadline, a peak period ahead → think it over or wrong time; an approver never on a call, new names, "I'll need to run it by..." or a client asking why some group is not in the room → check with someone, and a missing role for a criterion (2.3); an in-house admin who maintains the current setup or earlier DIY attempts → build it ourselves, whereas a builder who built a prototype, hit a wall and named the wall is a buying signal, not an objection; other vendors mentioned or a tender in research → other partners; early requests for pricing or a document → send a proposal; frustration with the platform → platform is the problem; a past failed rollout or low usage → adoption; the client naming a cost of changing what we built → changing what you built for us costs us; the same concern restated three times in a minute → restated and unanswered; the attendee list gaining people who have not seen the product → the meeting type will be evicted (4.11), which prep protects rather than answers.

## 6. After the call

### 6.1 Follow-up rules
The transcript is processed the same day. No meeting closes without a next step the client assented to (R10); if missing, the summary flags it, the recap draft proposes a concrete time, and a mechanism without a date emits a same-day date-proposal to-do. The recap is a draft the user sends; the invite stays a to-do until the user confirms it went. Commitments carry an owner and due date, including every question our side answered with a deferral or an unverified hedge, each converted to an owned, dated commitment and escalated in the summary only where it collides with a recorded blocker (C82). No proposal or statement-of-work draft without R12 evidence. Seeds and out-of-scope asks go to `backlog.md` in the client's words; demo asks go to `gaps.md` with their state. Pains confirmed in a demo are marked with the quote and the presenter's confirming sentence; mechanism-confirmed pains are marked as such. Answered questions leave the open list. **The correction loop:** anything said wrong or unsourced on the call becomes an entry on the next meeting's correction list, which the next run-sheet places before its first segment (7.1).

### 6.2 Support and check-in calls: the short instrument (method §6.4)
`support` and `check-in` calls are graded with five items instead of a fresh discovery grade; the cumulative R statuses are carried alongside, not re-derived from the call. A missing item becomes a reminder in the next check-in prep. The rows are written exactly as the s10 key writes them:

| Row | Met when the user... |
|---|---|
| `Support check 1 Solved their issue first` | dealt with the reported problem before anything else, diagnosing with questions rather than guesses |
| `Support check 2 Named what the fix prevents, in plain terms` | said what the fix stops from happening, in the client's frame and without the internal figure behind it |
| `Support check 3 Asked one forward-looking question before ending` | asked, once and near the end, "With this sorted, what's still being done by hand?" and stayed with the answer without turning the call into discovery |
| `Support check 4 Logged the answer to backlog.md as a seed` | recorded the need in the client's words as a seed — not scoped, priced or built |
| `Support check 5 Closed with a short note on what is worth keeping an eye on` | ended with one or two concrete, client-owned watch items |

A reported problem that turns out to be user error is diagnosis and explanation, not a defect, a change request or a build. A need that surfaces in passing is logged as an expansion seed and nothing more.

## 7. Never in client-facing material

**Client-facing means:** `sop/`, `deliverables/`, anything written into the client's monday account (workspace, board and column names, descriptions, docs, item names, seed data, updates, dashboards, forms, and under A17 the prompt text of an agent or Vibe app), any message the user will send, and anything on a shared screen, which no lint can see (`safety.md`).

**Never include:** (1) bleed and upside numbers, their inputs (hours, loaded costs, salaries) and the math; (2) emotional or personal stakes; (3) objections, raised or anticipated, and planned responses; (4) readiness grades, gaps, gap questions, gate status; (5) stakeholder assessments — decision power, what each person cares about, attitudes, politics, who is a risk; (6) closing tactics — deadline anchors, cost-of-waiting math, planned commitment questions, exit-ramp notes; (7) pricing strategy, rates, margins, discount thinking; (8) alternatives in play and what the client said about them; (9) research inferences and the evidence markers; (10) demo scripts, prep blocks, call summaries, raw transcripts, recording links, `log.md` content; (11) remarks by client staff about colleagues; (12) other clients' names, data or examples; (13) method vocabulary — bleed, upside, callback, exit ramp, seed, readiness, pain ids; (14) backlog items framed as sales opportunities (a neutral "possible next improvements" list is fine when the user asks); (15) maintenance promises — "set it and forget it" and every equivalent — and any automation described as maintenance-free (C47); (16) usage statistics, adoption percentages, client counts, other-client anecdotes, sector-experience claims and demand statistics about our own book, including in a self-introduction; the substitute is one verifiable credential, and in a partner-led seat with nobody to vouch that slot stays empty for the user to fill (C49, L18); (17) a vendor's data-handling, training, retention or compliance position asserted as ours, or a third party's such claim restated as ours — point at the vendor's published documentation and let the client's security review own it (C48); (18) our own operational data — engagement names, staff, hours, billable flags, rates or where they sit — on any screen or in any file; (19) a client's personal login as an access option, including as a hypothetical; (20) a permission described as verified when the audit could only record it as not checkable — it is described as configured.

**May contain:** agreed goals and requirements in neutral wording and the client's vocabulary; the solution as built; how-to steps and process diagrams; agreed success criteria without money figures; agreed next steps, dates and owners; the client's own SOP as the starting point for ours, reusing its vocabulary and structure and showing what changed (A16).

**The leak check** runs before any client-facing file is written or anything is written into monday (`leak_lint.py`; terms in `leak-terms.txt`): a block list built from the record's numbers in every rendering, objection text, stakeholder assessments, alternatives, method vocabulary, pain ids and markers; volume numbers that belong in an SOP are flagged, not removed. **An authoring rule the lint cannot enforce:** outside a call folder, tool references are category words, and no more than one category is named per sentence — the combination of a client's tools identifies them even with every proper noun removed (C54).

### 7.1 Claims discipline in live speech

The most damaging claims in the evidence base were improvised answers to live questions, which no lint reaches, so the discipline reaches speech through the prep pack, the register (`house-style.md`) and the correction loop; the store, the prep read and the audience routing are `safety.md`'s. (C45, C46, C100, C101, C102)

- **No figure without a source.** Every figure spoken was read live at prep with its date or came from the user; an unsourced figure is answered with a refusal, never a range, because a range spoken aloud becomes the remembered price. The "numbers we do not have" card names each category with no sourced figure and the sentence to say instead.
- **A commitment to check is exclusive of a figure in the same turn.** If the answer says we will confirm something, it carries no number, and the commitment is emitted as an owned to-do with a name and a date.
- **"It can" is not "I have."** Only *shown* and *buildable* capabilities are stated as capability; *unverified* is "I'd want to check that before I promise it", followed by the offer to build the smallest working version, run it once and measure. A plan-gated answer carries its gate; absent a recorded plan, the answer is that it depends on their tier and we will confirm which side of the line they are on.
- **No estimate for unbuilt or unread work** — no effort, duration or completion figure before scoping, on any call where the person who will price the work is present. The replacement is what has been seen so far, what still needs looking at before a number is put on it, and the artefact required first. No figure at all for work on a client-built artefact until we can read it (2.3).
- **No capability claim about a system nobody on our side has read.** Record what the client's own systems owner said, quoted and attributed; generate no capability, limit or flexibility statement of our own until the documentation has been read.
- **Never "we can change it later" unqualified.** Say what a conversion does and does not restore, grounded in our own probes (C92).
- **Commercial and account-status statements are routed by who is in the room** (4.10): none after the approval owner has left, none in a group recap, one named owner in a co-sell seat. **No leading price question**, and no reasoning from one vendor's pricing to another's.
- **A figure or promise inherited from a colleague's earlier meeting** is verified with that colleague before it is repeated. A third party's claim made beside us is recorded verbatim, attributed and dated, never restated as ours, and the follow-up points at the published documentation.
- **The never-say block** beside the platform gotchas (`monday-gotchas.md`) is read at prep and applied in the room; no client-facing script claims a capability from the AI-agent or Vibe family that has no gotcha entry (A17).
- **The correction loop.** After the call, the delivery notes and the deferred-answer register flag every unsourced figure, unverified capability, hedged answer and second-hand promise that was spoken. Each becomes an owned to-do or an entry on the next meeting's correction list, which the next run-sheet places before its first segment, so the correction is made in the room, attributed to us, and logged. The claims store catches contradictions across calls at prep; contradictions minutes apart inside one call are caught only by the rules above.
